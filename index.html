Is Osaka even the right call?

Fair question — you asked from scratch, so it's worth answering honestly instead of assuming. I weighed it against the two other realistic winter picks from Korea: Fukuoka (the shortest hop) and Hokkaido (the only one with actual snow).

Note: the table below is the original Osaka-only comparison from when this dossier started. The plan has since grown to include Tokyo + Mt Fuji (§07) — see §05 for the current, up-to-date budget.

Destination Winter flight, r/t What you get ≈ Per person, 4N5D
Osaka / Kansai this plan ₩230k–320k Castle, old-town Kyoto, Nara's deer, Dotonbori food crawl, USJ — the most "bucket list" density per yen, plus two easy day trips. ₩800k–950k
Fukuoka ₩200k–300k Shortest flight of the three (~1h). Food- and onsen-led — Dazaifu, Yufuin/Beppu hot springs — quieter, but far fewer headline sights. ₩800k–1.1M
Hokkaido / Sapporo ₩500k–800k The only one with real snow — skiing, the Snow Festival, proper winter cold. Genuinely a different trip, not a variant of the same one. ₩1.3M–1.8M+
💭 My honest take: stick with Osaka. Given "cheaper is better" has been the brief the whole way through, it's genuinely the best value of the three — cheapest flights, and Kyoto + Nara give you more range than Fukuoka's day trips for similar or less money. Fukuoka's only real edge is an even shorter flight — worth it only if you'd rather eat and soak than sightsee. Hokkaido is the one honest upgrade: it's real winter, snow included, which Osaka's 5–12°C simply isn't. But budget roughly doubles, and the Snow Festival itself runs Feb 4–11, 2027 — right on top of Seollal, so you'd be stacking two price surges at once. If a "real winter" trip is the actual goal, that's worth planning as its own trip later with a bigger budget, not squeezed into this one.

Getting there — flights

ICN → KIX is one of the most heavily served short-haul routes out of Korea, so the LCCs undercut the flag carriers by a wide margin. Book roughly 6–8 weeks out on a weekday for the fares below.

Compare across all of these at once on Naver Flight, Skyscanner or Trip.com rather than one airline's own site — LCC seat sales don't always show up until you filter by date.

Also worth checking — flying out of Gimpo (GMP) instead

Gimpo does run a Kansai route: Korean Air, Asiana and Jeju Air each fly it twice daily, plus Peach four times daily — about 14 departures a day combined, same ~1h45m flight. Off-peak, with an airline coupon, fares can dip into the high ₩100,000s per person — in the same ballpark as the Incheon LCCs above.

The real draw isn't the ticket price, it's the airport itself: Gimpo sits inside the city, ~20–30 min from most of Seoul, versus 60–90 min out to Incheon. For a group of 6 that's a real chunk of time and transfer cost saved, especially on an early flight straight after your last paper.

The trade-off — T'way, the cheapest carrier in the table above, doesn't fly this route, and departure times cluster mid-morning to evening rather than spreading out, so there's less room to chase an ultra-cheap red-eye.

🧭 My take: if any of you actually live near Gimpo or central Seoul, check Jeju Air / Peach on GMP–KIX before booking — the time and transfer money saved can outweigh a slightly higher fare. Purely on ticket price alone, though, Incheon still has the edge, since it has more carriers fighting for your seat.

Kereta sewa — yes, for the Fuji leg

The plan: rent a 7–8 seater in Tokyo on Saturday morning, drive to Kawaguchiko for the day, drive back to the Tokyo apartment that night, and return the car on Sunday morning. It's a round trip from Tokyo, so there's no one-way drop-off fee, which is what killed the car idea before. It costs a little more than the bus, but you get the lakes and Oshino Hakkai on your own schedule.

Who drives

Three of the group have Malaysian licences and take turns. Each driver must show passport + original Malaysian licence + Malaysian IDP (JPJ, RM150, the 1949 Geneva booklet) at the counter. No IDP, no car. From Korea, apply in the MyJPJ app (needs MyDigital ID), have it couriered to family in Malaysia, then posted to Korea. That takes weeks, so start now. The licence must still be valid for at least a year when you apply.

⚠️ The drive back is at night. You leave Kawaguchiko around 18:30 after Isyak, in the dark, at below-zero temperatures, so roads can have black ice. Swap drivers every hour or so, stop at Dangozaka SA if anyone is drowsy, and don't push on tired. The route — Osaka ⇄ Tokyo ⇄ Mt Fuji, 6 nights 7 days

Osaka first, an overnight bus up to Tokyo and two nights in an apartment there, a rental-car day trip out to Kawaguchiko for Fuji, a Sunday in Tokyo, and an overnight bus down to Osaka, skipping the shinkansen entirely. A full Kyoto/Nara/USJ day didn't fit without cutting Tokyo or Fuji short, so Kyoto gets a half-day on Day 6 for Kiyomizu-dera. Full airport-to-airport schedule with exact times, every meal and every solat slot is in the companion Excel file — this is the condensed version.

1
Wed 6 Jan — Land 09:15, full day in Namba
Confirmed flight lands 09:15am — a whole day, not just an evening. Immigration + bags, Nankai Airport Express to Namba (¥970, ~45min — regular express, not Rapi:t). Kuromon Market for lunch, guesthouse check-in from ~2pm, unhurried Shinsaibashi/Namba afternoon, then Dotonbori at night — Glico sign, canal, a kushikatsu crawl. Halal lunch + dinner near Namba since the guesthouse kitchen isn't in play until you're checked in.

Cook breakfast at the guesthouse (kitchen confirmed). Pack up, and at 23:00 board the overnight bus to Tokyo (Willer Express, ¥2,000–4,000, ~8–9h, sleep on the bus). Solat note: Subuh (05:38) and Isyak (18:20) both land while you're still out sightseeing — plan around Zohor/Asar at the castle park or a quiet corner near Umeda.

Bus lands Shinjuku ~07:30. Freshen up at the terminal, halal breakfast (onigiri/bread), then Asakusa Senso-ji (free). JR from Ōtsuka to Shinjuku (~10 min) and pick up the car at Toyota Rent a Car Shinjuku at 08:00 (passport + licence + IDP for all 3 drivers). Chuo Expressway to Kawaguchiko IC: ~1h30m if clear, expect 2h+ since it's the start of a 3-day weekend. Chureito Pagoda first while Fuji is clear, hoto lunch at T&T Fujiyama Halal, Zohor at Fujikawaguchiko Masjid, then Oishi Park and Oshino Hakkai. Maghrib, halal dinner, then the 23:00 Willer night bus from Busta Shinjuku to Osaka (~¥3,000–5,000; book early, it's a holiday weekend). Solat: Zohor at Masjid Nusantara (Akihabara); Asar and Maghrib at the Shibuya PARCO 5F prayer room.

Leave ~10:30: Midosuji Line Namba → Yodoyabashi (¥240), then Keihan Line → Kiyomizu-Gojo (¥430), ~1h15m total. Zohor + halal lunch at Shinjuku-tei Kiyomizu near Ninenzaka (halal wagyu ramen, with a prayer space; opens 12:00; chicken paitan ¥1,500–2,500, wagyu ramen ¥4,500). Walk up Ninenzaka/Sannenzaka to Kiyomizu-dera (¥500, cash, open 06:00–18:00), Asar nearby, then head back ~15:30 so you're in Namba by Maghrib. Cook dinner with the leftover groceries and pack early — tomorrow's flight is brutal. Heads up: Jan 11, 2027 is a Japanese public holiday (Coming of Age Day), so expect Kyoto to be busier than a normal Monday. The last stretch from Kiyomizu-Gojo is a steep ~25-minute uphill walk; if the group is wiped after two overnight buses, a taxi split six ways is cheap. Skipping Kyoto and just resting is still a fine call.

Tue 12 Jan — Fly out, 07:40

⚠️ The overnight buses are the real challenge. Both of them (Osaka→Tokyo on Day 2, Tokyo→Osaka on Day 5) cross a Subuh window while still moving. Bring water/tayammum-safe wipes, a qiblat app, and don't be afraid to pray seated or to jamak/qada once you land if it genuinely isn't possible on board.
Where the halal food actually is

Osaka: Gyomu Super has several halal-stocked branches (Nippombashi, Minamitsumori, Bentencho, Momodani) — meat, rice, spices, snacks; Halal Ramen Naniwaya near Namba does bento from ¥1,200 and a wagyu rice bowl around ¥3,000.

Tokyo: Tokyo Camii (Yoyogi-Uehara) is a full mosque with a halal market and Turkish food attached — the plan for Friday Jumaat on Day 3. Shin-Okubo also has a cluster of halal-friendly Turkish/Middle Eastern spots.

Kawaguchiko: better than it used to be. Fujikawaguchiko Masjid (opened 2020) has a halal café in the same building; T&T Fujiyama Halal Restaurant does halal hoto near Chureito; Idaten Bettei by the station is halal with a prayer room. Still carry a backup snack, since small places fill up on weekends.

🍳 On "masak je supaya jimat": honest answer — cooking only realistically works on the Osaka guesthouse nights (Day 2 and Day 6, kitchen confirmed). Days 3–5 you're out all day in Tokyo or on the road (ask 9STAY whether the apartment has a kitchen; if it does, breakfasts there are a bonus), so those meals are mostly eat-out regardless of intent. Budget-wise that's roughly 40% cooked, 60% bought — still worth doing (₩16,200/person in groceries instead of eating out for those same meals), just don't expect it to cover the whole week.

Book these now — before seats/slots run out

Most of this trip is walk-up (temples, markets, Dotonbori). A handful of things aren't — they're either capacity-limited (a bus has a fixed number of seats) or timed-entry (a ticket for a specific hour). Miss the window on these and you're either stuck, or paying a lot more for a same-day scramble. Here's the honest priority order.

Priority 1 — book this week

The two overnight buses (Willer Express Osaka→Tokyo, Thu 7 Jan; Tokyo→Osaka, Sun 10 Jan), the rental car, and the drivers' IDPs

These aren't optional add-ons — they're your transport and your accommodation for those nights. If either sells out, you're not just missing a fun extra, you're stuck rebuilding the whole route (extra hotel night, more expensive shinkansen, knock-on schedule changes for everyone).

Japan highway bus operators typically open bookings around 3 months before departure — for a Jan 6–12 trip, that window is opening right about now (late Sept/early Oct). Two things stack risk on top of that: Jan 6–12 sits right at the tail end of Japan's New Year travel rush (many locals are still moving around through the first week of Jan, and Japanese school winter break often runs into early/mid-Jan), and Sun 10 Jan is the middle of a 3-day weekend, so both Tokyo→Osaka bus seats and 7–8 seater rental cars get tight.

Rental car: book a W2-class (Noah/Voxy) with winter tyres and an ETC card, Sat 9 Jan 08:00 → Sun 10 Jan ~11:00 in Shinjuku. IDPs: the 3 drivers need their Malaysian IDP booklets in hand before the trip. From Korea that means MyJPJ, a courier to Malaysia and then post, which takes weeks, so start this now.

✅ Worth it? Not really a question — these ARE the plan, not an optional extra. Book via willer-travel.com (English site, card payment) as soon as your headcount and dates are locked. Book all 6 seats in one go if the site allows it, so you're guaranteed to sit together.
Priority 2 — book 1–2 weeks ahead

Shibuya Sky (Day 5, listed as optional)

It's a timed-entry ticket, not a walk-up queue — you book a specific hour through the official site. Current pricing: ¥2,700 (~₩24,300) online before 3pm, ¥3,400 (~₩30,600) from 3pm onward, +¥300 more if you buy at the counter instead of online (assuming there's anything left to buy). Sunset slots sell out first, but in January sunset (~16:45) lands right on Maghrib, and they're the pricier after-3pm tier, so the plan uses a 14:45 slot instead. Tickets release 14 days ahead at midnight (Korea and Japan share a time zone), which is 27 Dec for the Sunday (Day 5) visit.

💭 Worth it? Yes, if it survives the budget trim — 360° open-air Tokyo view with Fuji visible on a clear winter day is genuinely one of the better skyline views in the city, better than most indoor observation decks for the same price bracket. If you're doing it, book the 14:45 slot online as soon as tickets release on 27 Dec, not the morning of. If it gets cut for budget, no regrets — it's correctly flagged as the first thing to drop in §05.
Priority 3 — optional, consider adding

teamLab Planets or Borderless (not currently in the itinerary)

Two separate venues now: teamLab Planets (Toyosu — barefoot, walk-through water rooms, structured route) and teamLab Borderless (Azabudai Hills — shoes-on, open wander, no fixed path). Dynamic pricing from about ¥3,600 (~₩32,400) per adult, rising on busy days. Planets tickets are never sold at the door — online only, always. Borderless sometimes has same-day counter tickets if inventory's left, at a small surcharge. January is off-peak compared to sakura/Golden Week/summer, so sellout risk is lower than the "book weeks ahead" warnings you'll see online, but weekend slots (your Day 4, a Saturday) still go first.

🧭 Worth it? If anyone in the group hasn't done a teamLab before, it's a genuinely different kind of attraction — immersive digital art, very photogenic, not "just another museum." The honest catch: it's not currently budgeted, and Day 5 only has room for one big timed-entry thing alongside everything else planned. If it replaces Shibuya Sky rather than stacking on top, it's a wash on both time and cost — worth a group vote on which one people actually want more.
Done ✓ — no longer needed

Manga cafe booths, Tokyo (replaced by the 9STAY apartment)

Individual booths rarely "sell out" city-wide, but a specific branch can run short on private/group booths on a Friday or Saturday night in a busy area like Shinjuku or Shibuya — exactly your Day 3/4 nights. Most chains (Gran Cyber Cafe, Media Cafe Popeye, Manboo) let you reserve online or by phone.

💭 Worth it? Reserve the branch and booth type (flat-mat rooms fit a group better than single reclining seats) a few days ahead rather than the same day — low effort, removes a real "where do we sleep tonight" risk for 6 people arriving off an overnight bus.

Everything else in the itinerary — Osaka Castle, Kuromon Market, Dotonbori, Asakusa, Tokyo Camii, the Kawaguchiko Fuji viewpoints — is free or walk-up, no booking risk. The Osaka Amazing Pass (§05) is sold at machines/stations same-day and doesn't sell out.

Ways to trim it further

01

Fly Tue/Wed/Thu. Weekend departures from ICN run noticeably higher on every LCC — shifting either leg by a day can save ₩20,000–40,000 a head.

02

Skip the rail pass entirely. With Tokyo↔Osaka now running on overnight buses instead of shinkansen (§05/§07), a JR pass wouldn't pay for itself — a rechargeable ICOCA/Suica for city metro is all you need on top of the bus tickets.

03

Conbini breakfast on the days you must eat out. Halal-safe picks (salmon/tuna onigiri, bread) run ¥600–800 versus ¥1,500+ for a sit-down café — over the Tokyo/travel mornings for 6 people that's a real line item, not a rounding error.

04

Confirm the room before you pay, not after. A single "sleeps 6" listing is how you got this price — message the host for photos of the actual bedding setup so nobody's finding out about the floor situation at midnight on Jan 6.
