Day 1 · Wed 6 Jan · Osaka

Osaka — Minami (Namba)

First day, fresh off the plane. Everything is walkable around Namba, so no passes needed yet.

Food market

Kuromon Market 黒門市場

7/10

A 580-metre covered market that has been feeding Osaka since the Edo period, nicknamed “Osaka's kitchen”. Around 150 stalls sell fresh seafood, fruit and street snacks grilled right in front of you.

Why it's worth it: The easiest first taste of Japanese food culture: scallops, crab legs and fruit you can eat standing up, straight after landing.
Cost
¥500–2,000 grazing
Time needed
1–1.5 h
Hours
~09:00–17:00 (stalls vary)
Best time
Late morning, before stalls pack up
  • Prices are tourist-level. Compare a few stalls before buying.
  • Most stalls are not halal-certified. Plain grilled seafood and fruit are the safe picks; ask about sauces (they often contain mirin or sake), and skip the wagyu skewers.
  • Many stalls close by 5pm, so go when you arrive, not in the evening.
Halal & solatPlain seafood and fruit here; proper halal lunch around Namba (see Dotonbori).
Shopping street

Shinsaibashi-suji 心斎橋筋商店街

6/10

A long covered shopping arcade running north from Dotonbori, packed with drugstores, fashion chains, snack shops and Don Quijote nearby.

Why it's worth it: It's roofed, so it's warm and dry in January. Good for a slow first afternoon and cheap souvenir snacks.
Cost
Free (shopping extra)
Time needed
1–2 h
Hours
Most shops ~10:00–21:00
Best time
Afternoon
  • Drugstores here are the cheapest place for Japanese snacks and skincare.
  • Japan's tax-free system changed on 1 Nov 2026: you now pay full price and claim the 10% back at the airport. Keep receipts together (see Prep).
Halal & solatPrayer room at Namba City mall, right by Namba station.
Night district

Dotonbori 道頓堀

9/10

Osaka's famous canal strip of giant neon signs, mechanical crabs and street food. The Glico “Running Man” sign has been on this canal since 1935 and is the classic Osaka photo.

Why it's worth it: This is Osaka. The city's motto is kuidaore, “eat until you drop”, and Dotonbori at night is where it happens.
Cost
Free (food extra)
Time needed
2–3 h
Hours
Always open; best after dark
Best time
After 18:00, lights on
  • Best Glico photo spot: Ebisu Bridge (戎橋).
  • Takoyaki is octopus, but stalls aren't halal-certified. Ask about the sauce, or eat at a certified place instead.
  • Kushikatsu shops mostly fry pork alongside everything else. Choose a halal restaurant instead.
Halal & solatCHIBO Diversity Dotonbori (Muslim-friendly okonomiyaki, with a prayer room) and Ali's Kitchen (halal Pakistani, Shinsaibashi).

Day 2 · Thu 7 Jan · Osaka

Osaka — Castle, Umeda & Shinsekai

Amazing Pass day (¥3,500), which covers metro rides plus the three big stops below. Overnight bus to Tokyo leaves at 23:00.

History

Osaka Castle 大阪城

8/10

Built in 1583 by Toyotomi Hideyoshi, the warlord who unified Japan. The white-and-gold main tower you see is a 1931 reconstruction, now an 8-floor museum with a lookout on top, surrounded by huge stone walls, moats and a park.

Why it's worth it: The most iconic building in Osaka, and the park is great for a slow walk and a quiet Zohor.
Cost
¥1,200 — free with Amazing Pass
Time needed
2 h
Hours
09:00–17:00 (last entry 16:30)
Best time
Opening time, before tour groups
  • Honestly, the outside and the grounds are the best part. The museum inside is crowded and very text-heavy.
  • Lift queues are long, so take the stairs down.
  • Nishinomaru Garden has the classic view of the tower.
Halal & solatPray in a quiet corner of the castle park (bring a sejadah).
Viewpoint

Umeda Sky Building 梅田スカイビル

7/10

Two 173-metre towers joined at the top by a ring-shaped “Floating Garden” observatory, with an open-air rooftop walk. You ride up in a glass-tube escalator crossing the gap between the towers.

Why it's worth it: 360° view over Osaka, and the escalator ride alone is worth it.
Cost
Free with Amazing Pass before 15:00
Time needed
1 h
Hours
Daily, into the evening
Best time
Before 15:00 (pass cut-off)
  • Be there before 3pm. After that the Amazing Pass only gives 10% off.
  • The open rooftop is windy and cold in January, so wear gloves.
Halal & solatPlenty of halal options around Umeda/Osaka Station if lunch runs late.
Retro district

Tsutenkaku & Shinsekai 通天閣・新世界

7/10

Shinsekai (“New World”) is a retro 1910s-era neighbourhood of bright signs and old-school eateries, centred on the 108-metre Tsutenkaku Tower. The tower's mascot is Billiken, a smiling figure whose feet people rub for luck.

Why it's worth it: Feels completely different from modern Namba: loud, nostalgic and very photogenic at dusk.
Cost
Tower free with Amazing Pass
Time needed
1.5 h
Hours
Tower ~10:00–20:00
Best time
Dusk, as the lights come on
  • Shinsekai is the birthplace of kushikatsu, but most shops use pork and serve alcohol, so eat at Seyhan Kebab instead.
  • Stick to the main lit streets at night.
Halal & solatSeyhan Kebab (listed on Google Maps as “Seyhan Kebab tennoji shop”), a Turkish halal restaurant in Ebisuhigashi, a short walk from the tower. It's rated 4.7 and good for dinner before the bus.

Day 3 · Fri 8 Jan · Tokyo

Tokyo — Asakusa, Tokyo Camii, Shibuya

Arrive at Shinjuku bus terminal ~07:30. It's Friday, so the men should plan the day around Solat Jumaat at Tokyo Camii.

Temple

Senso-ji 浅草寺

9/10

Tokyo's oldest temple, founded in 628. You enter through Kaminarimon (“Thunder Gate”) with its giant red lantern, then walk Nakamise-dori, a 250-metre street of traditional snack and souvenir stalls, up to the main hall and five-storey pagoda.

Why it's worth it: The best “old Tokyo” atmosphere in the city, it's free, and a great first stop straight off the bus.
Cost
Free
Time needed
1.5–2 h
Hours
Grounds always open; main hall 06:30–17:00 (Oct–Mar)
Best time
Before 10:00, fewer crowds
  • Things like incense, fortune slips (omikuji) and ringing bells are optional. You can simply walk around as a visitor.
  • Nakamise snacks: try ningyo-yaki (small cakes with red bean filling) and check the ingredients as usual.
  • Eating while walking is discouraged here. Stand by the stall to eat.
Halal & solatShinjuku-tei Asakusa (halal wagyu ramen, with a prayer room, 2 minutes from Asakusa station). It opens at 12:00, so on this Friday it suits a return visit rather than lunch. Naritaya, the older halal ramen shop here, has closed permanently.
Mosque

Tokyo Camii 東京ジャーミイ

10/10

Japan's largest mosque. The original was built in 1938 by Tatar Muslims who had settled in Tokyo; the current Ottoman-style building was completed in 2000 using Turkish architects, craftsmen and materials. It also houses a Turkish culture centre and a halal grocery.

Why it's worth it: For this group it's a must: a proper place for Jumaat, a stunning interior, and halal food under the same roof.
Cost
Free
Time needed
1.5 h (with Jumaat)
Hours
10:00–18:00 daily
Best time
Friday for Jumaat
  • Friday: azan ~12:30, Jumaat ~13:00, with the khutbah in Japanese, Turkish and English. Men should arrive by 12:15.
  • The itinerary is built around this: leave Senso-ji ~11:30 (~40 min via Ginza → Chiyoda line), lunch after Jumaat at ~13:30.
  • Nearest station: Yoyogi-Uehara (Chiyoda/Odakyu line), a 5-minute walk.
Halal & solatThe halal market on the ground floor sells Turkish bread and snacks, which make good snacks for the bus and Kawaguchiko.
City icon

Shibuya Crossing & Hachiko 渋谷スクランブル交差点・ハチ公

8/10

The famous scramble crossing, where every direction goes green at once and hundreds of people cross together. Next to it is the statue of Hachiko, the dog who waited at Shibuya Station every day for nearly 10 years after his owner died in 1925.

Why it's worth it: The most “Tokyo” moment you can have in five minutes, for free.
Cost
Free
Time needed
30–60 min
Hours
Always
Best time
After dark, when the screens are lit
  • For the view from above for free, use the walkway window in Shibuya Mark City (between the station and Mark City).
  • The crossing is heaving on Friday and Saturday nights, so keep bags in front.
Halal & solatPrayer room on 5F of Shibuya PARCO.
Street fashion

Harajuku & Takeshita Street 原宿・竹下通り

6/10

A narrow 350-metre street that's the centre of Tokyo's youth fashion and kawaii culture: colourful shops, rainbow snacks and famous crepe stands. Meiji Jingu's forested shrine grounds are right next door.

Why it's worth it: Fun and very photogenic, but it's crowded and short, so treat it as a quick stop.
Cost
Free
Time needed
1 h
Hours
Shops ~10:00–20:00
Best time
Weekday afternoon
  • Crepe fillings can include gelatin or liqueur, so ask first.
  • For a calm contrast, walk the gravel path through the Meiji Jingu forest (free, closes around sunset).
Halal & solatShibuya PARCO prayer room is one stop away.

Day 4 · Sat 9 Jan · Mt Fuji

Mt Fuji by car — Chureito, lakes & a night by the lake

Pick up the car in Shinjuku at 08:00 (3 drivers: passport + Malaysian licence + IDP) and drive ~1.5–2.5 h to Kawaguchiko. There's halal food and a mosque here now. Tonight is in the car at Michi-no-eki Katsuyama, so read the safety box.

Viewpoint

Chureito Pagoda 忠霊塔

10/10

A red five-storey pagoda on the hillside of Arakurayama Sengen Park, built in 1963 as a peace memorial. From the lookout above it, the pagoda and Mt Fuji line up in one frame: the postcard shot of Japan.

Why it's worth it: This is the photo everyone pictures when they think of Japan, and winter gives the clearest Fuji views of the year.
Cost
Free
Time needed
1.5–2 h
Hours
Always open
Best time
Morning; clouds often cover Fuji by afternoon
  • 398 stone steps up (10–15 minutes). They can be icy in January, so wear shoes with grip.
  • By car, park at the Arakurayama Sengen Park lot. It fills up on weekends, so this is the first stop of the day.
  • Go here first, while Fuji is still clear.
Halal & solatT&T Fujiyama Halal Restaurant in Fujiyoshida, near the pagoda: halal hoto (Yamanashi's thick miso noodle stew), all ¥1,000, open 11:30–19:30. A hot bowl after 398 cold steps.
Lake view

Lake Kawaguchiko & Oishi Park 河口湖・大石公園

8/10

The most accessible of the Fuji Five Lakes. Oishi Park on the north shore looks straight across the water to Mt Fuji, with the reflection on calm days.

Why it's worth it: A relaxed, free afternoon with Fuji in front of you the whole time. It's also where Maghrib at sunset over Fuji could happen.
Cost
Free
Time needed
1–2 h
Hours
Always open
Best time
Afternoon to sunset
  • Come back at sunrise on Sunday (~06:50): calmest water and clearest Fuji of the whole trip.
  • Temperatures are below zero by evening, so layer up.
Halal & solatFujikawaguchiko Masjid (opened 2020, near Kawaguchiko station) has a halal café in the same building, so do Maghrib, dinner and Isyak there. Idaten Bettei by the station is also halal, with a prayer room.
Spring ponds

Oshino Hakkai 忍野八海

8/10

Eight ponds in a small village between Lake Kawaguchiko and Lake Yamanakako, fed by Mt Fuji snowmelt that filters through layers of lava rock for decades. The water is clear enough to see the bottom metres down, with thatched-roof houses and Fuji behind.

Why it's worth it: Awkward by bus but easy by car, free, and a completely different Fuji scene from the lakes.
Cost
Free (one pond is inside a small museum, ~¥300)
Time needed
1 h
Hours
Ponds open 24 h; shops ~09:00–17:00
Best time
Late afternoon, after the tour buses
  • You can fill a bottle with the spring water for free, and it's safe to drink.
  • About 25 minutes' drive from Lake Kawaguchiko.
  • Snack stalls here aren't halal-certified, so save dinner for the masjid café.
Halal & solatHead back to Fujikawaguchiko Masjid for Maghrib and dinner at its halal café.

Safety · night in the car

🚗 Sleeping in the car at −7°C: the rules

  • Park at Michi-no-eki Katsuyama on the lakeshore: free parking and 24-hour toilets. Resting overnight at roadside stations is tolerated; don't set anything up outside the car.
  • The average January low here is −7°C, and it can drop to −10°C. Bring sleeping bags rated to −10°C, plus kairo heat packs from any konbini.
  • Engine off while everyone sleeps. If you run the heater, one person stays awake. Snow can block the exhaust and push carbon monoxide into the car. People die this way in Japan most winters. Bring a battery CO detector.
  • Never use a gas stove or burner inside the car. Crack a window about 1 cm to keep air moving.
  • Whoever drives on Sunday morning gets the best seat to sleep in. Tired driving on an icy road is the biggest risk of the whole plan.
  • If it's snowing hard or anyone feels unwell, go to a hostel. It isn't worth the risk.

Shibuya Sky 渋谷スカイ

8/10

An open-air rooftop 229 metres up on Shibuya Scramble Square, with glass edges and no roof, giving a 360° view over Tokyo. On a clear winter day you can see Mt Fuji.

Why it's worth it: Probably the best skyline view in Tokyo. Worth it if the budget allows; the first thing to cut if it doesn't.

Day 6 · Mon 11 Jan · Kyoto

Kyoto — Kiyomizu-dera half-day

Rest in the morning after the bus, then Kyoto from ~10:30. It's a Japanese public holiday (Coming of Age Day), so expect crowds.

Old town

Ninenzaka & Sannenzaka 二年坂・三年坂

8/10

Two sloping stone-paved lanes lined with wooden machiya townhouses, tea shops and craft stores. This is the path up to Kiyomizu-dera and the best-preserved old-Kyoto streetscape.

Why it's worth it: It's the Kyoto you see in photos, and you walk through it on the way to the temple anyway.
Cost
Free
Time needed
45 min
Hours
Shops ~09:00–17:00
Best time
Late morning
  • Local legend says if you trip on Sannenzaka you'll have three years of bad luck, so watch your step on the stairs.
  • Kyoto asks visitors not to eat while walking in these lanes.
Halal & solatShinjuku-tei Kiyomizu (148 Hoshinocho, by Ninenzaka): halal wagyu ramen with a prayer space. Opens 12:00; chicken paitan ¥1,500–2,500, wagyu ramen ¥4,500.
Temple · UNESCO

Kiyomizu-dera 清水寺

9/10

Founded in 778 and part of the UNESCO “Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto”. Its main hall has a huge wooden stage about 13 metres above the hillside, rebuilt in 1633 without a single nail, looking out over Kyoto. Below it, the Otowa Waterfall splits into three streams.

Why it's worth it: One of the most famous sights in Japan, and the view from the stage over the city is spectacular.
Cost
¥500 (cash)
Time needed
1–1.5 h
Hours
06:00–18:00
Best time
Early afternoon
  • There's a Japanese saying, “jump off the stage of Kiyomizu”, meaning to take a big leap or bold decision.
  • Drinking from the Otowa stream is a ritual for good fortune. It's optional; just look if you prefer.
  • Bring cash: cards aren't reliably accepted at the gate.
Halal & solatBackup if Shinjuku-tei is full: Halal Ramen Honolu Premier in Gion (opens 11:30), about 15 minutes' walk down the hill.

Japan etiquette in 60 seconds

🚃
Trains are quiet

No phone calls, and keep your voice low. Queue in the marked lines on the platform.

🛗
Escalators

Osaka: stand on the right. Tokyo: stand on the left. Leave the other side free for walkers.

🗑️
Hardly any bins

Carry a small plastic bag for your trash. Konbini have bins inside.

💴
Cash still matters

Temples, market stalls and small eateries are often cash-only.

🙅
No tipping

Tipping isn't done in Japan and can confuse people. Just say “arigatou gozaimasu”.

⛩️
At temples

You're a visitor, not a worshipper: no need to bow, pray or do any ritual. Keep your voice down, follow photo signs, and take shoes off where asked (wear clean socks!).

🚶
Eating while walking

Frowned upon in Kyoto and Asakusa. Eat standing next to the stall.

🕌
Solat in public

Prayer rooms are listed on each card. Outdoors, choose a quiet corner out of the way. People are generally respectful.
